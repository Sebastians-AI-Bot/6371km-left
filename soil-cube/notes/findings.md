# Soil cube look

Recommendation: the mapped cube.

The reference is one block. Grass sits on top, and the two cut faces show dark soil, pale roots, and small stones. Cutting those three regions out of the photo and placing them on a cube puts each part back on the face it came from, so the object reads as that same block. Orbiting keeps the grass on top and the roots on the sides.

The slice stack is the front cut, six times, each shifted a little back. Roots and stones stay sharp, and the stepped edges read as thickness. It does not grow a grass top or a second cut, so it stays a fan of one photograph rather than the cube in the picture.

## What is on the page

Open `index.html` by double-clicking it. It runs from `file://` with ordinary image tags and one script. No server.

The switch is Both, Mapped cube, or Slice stack. Both shows the two ideas side by side. The other two make one of them larger and leave the other in view. Drag orbits. The scroll wheel zooms.

The cuts live next to the page as `face-top.png`, `face-front.png`, and `face-side.png`, taken from `reference.png`. The photo never shows the back or the far side, so those faces repeat a cut. The underside is darkened soil from the side cut, without a second copy of the root band.

## Limits

The top is softer than the photo. In the picture that face is a shallow band, and it has to be stretched into a square. The stack is the clearer view if the only goal is to read the roots on one cut.
